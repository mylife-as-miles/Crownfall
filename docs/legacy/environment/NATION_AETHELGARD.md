# Aethelgard — design sheet: the clan steading

*The Blood-Throne of Aethelgard — primordial bloodline nation.* First-pass layout

Maps: `maps/aethelgard-*.png`. All names are working placeholders.

## Terrain logic — a knoll bound to the wild

A glacial upland valley. A moraine knoll (≈ +12 m) stands on the north-west side of a river that
comes down from the mountains to the north-east. Its top is levelled at **+10 m** for the compound,
ringed by a palisade on an earth bank with a ditch. The **levy terrace at +5.5 m** sits on the
knoll's south shoulder, 4.5 m below the raised gate apron, reached by a ramp raking along the
rampart under the defenders' eyes. A graded way drops from the terrace to the **ford**, where the
river runs 0.9 m deep over gravel, and the valley road climbs the far bank north-east. Behind the
knoll a saddle (+7 m) holds the spring that never freezes; beyond it a steep forested **fell** climbs
60 m to glacier-scoured crags. A ring of standing stones raised by an older people stands on a ledge
cut into the fell's foot north-east of the compound (+10.5 m).

## Organisation — a clan compound, not a town

- **A Longhall Yard.** The ancestral longhall (40 × 11.5 m, curved keel roof of turf, carved gable
  boards worn smooth, the banner's shield over the door) faces a hearth yard where the fire is kept
  in every season, ringed by eight carved honour posts with bare iron hooks. The chief's house, the
  cook house, the guest-hall frame (posts and roof trees raised, scaffolded, waiting for staves) and
  an old ash behind the hall.
- **B Levy Terrace (+5.5 m).** A kerbed earthen muster mound older than the palisade on the valley
  edge, two standards, eight file stones a–h and the Pawn's stone before it; a turf levy house and a
  weapon house; the drill square; a split-rail fence along the valley edge.
- **C Family Yards.** Longhouses and pit-houses along the Yard Lane inside the west palisade: the
  elder's stave longhouse, a byre-end longhouse with cattle at one end, a weaver's longhouse, a
  founding cottage whose turf has not greened, a herder's pit-house, a pit-house with a fallen corner
  against the bank, an empty longhouse barred with the previous holders' mark, a smallholder's house.
- **D Craft Yards and Byre.** Smithy with an open forge shed, tannery against the palisade,
  smokehouse, staddle granary, woodwright's shop, and the great byre — built to a scale no herd of
  cattle needs, its door twice the longhall's height, with an arch of enormous ribs over the byre
  gate.
- **E South Gate and Herald's Station.** A timber gate tower on its raised apron; the herald's hut
  with carved message sticks pinned to posts, and the horn tower that answers the horn post at the
  ford.
- **F Record House.** The War Record: a carved-board record house joined to the longhall's east end
  by a covered passage; the previous holders' tallies run floor to roof, the banner's record begins
  on one new plank.
- **G Outer Territory.** Oat and barley strips running over the earlier holders' ploughed-out outer
  ring, a fallow strip, the hay meadow by the byre gate, the river mill, the ford horn post, a storm
  watch hut on the fell crest, a disputed boundary at the end of the field track.

## Dominant shapes and skyline

Long, low and heavy: the longhall's black keel roof and the great byre's curved roof inside a
palisade ring, the horn tower and the rebuilt watchtower as the only verticals, turf roofs that
grow out of the ground. Behind: the fell, the crags with great nesting ledges, glacier-hung
mountains to the north. Up the valley: the allied Great House's barrow-hall among colossal standing
stones on the upper shoulder (72°, 5.2 km), a dark crag hall on the eastern heights (96°, 6 km) and
the capital — a citadel of colossal dark stone on volcanic heights with a slow smoke plume
(60°, 26 km, always "the capital").

## Construction traditions

`ag-longhall-timber` (lordly and ancestral halls, record house, gate tower, granary),
`ag-turf-house` (dwellings, cook house, craft sheds), `ag-fieldstone-work` (the muster mound,
kerbs, standing stones). Parameters in `STYLE_GUIDE.md` §3. New work is pale oak among tarred black
staves; turf patches are bright green on old brown roofs.

## Life

Sentries on the south and north palisade walks, gate guards, the smith under the forge shed, a rider
of the Blood-Throne coming up the valley road and over the ford to the Herald's Station, the levy
drilling on the terrace, the herald at the message posts, the tally keeper crossing to the
longhall, a water carrier between the spring and the cook house, the weaver at the drying fence,
a herder leading cattle out of the byre gate, children round the hearth ring, the woodwright
carrying staves to the guest hall, farmers on the oat strips, a drover's cart at the ford.

## Atmosphere

Late afternoon in a cold upland: a low sun from the south-west (bearing 235°, elevation 14°) under a
broken cloud ceiling, mist pooling along the river. Black oak, green and red turf, pale new timber;
the hearth, the forge and torches as accents; the horn tower lamp lit when a rider is sighted. Fog is
a valley layer below the knoll, never a veil over the compound.
